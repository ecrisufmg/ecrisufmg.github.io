  • Listening/learning from/with machines: developing and applying flexible tools

    2024–ongoing · José Henrique Padovani · FAPEMIG/CNPq

    Programa Bolsas de Produtividade Estaduais

    abstract

    The project proposes the development of flexible tools — in terms of application in offline contexts (computer-assisted composition, investigations in sonology and musicology, etc.) and real-time contexts (interactive music systems, live electronics, real-time sound processing and synthesis, etc.) — of methods and strategies that combine (1) audio descriptors and related signal analysis/processing techniques and (2) neural networks and related machine learning techniques. The research strategy combines practice-oriented methodology (Practice-Based Research), with investigative and creative projects and their specificities guiding the techniques and tools to be developed, applied and assessed as to their effectiveness and general characteristics. The developed tools will involve the use of processing and analysis techniques based on temporal, spectral and psychoacoustic descriptors combined with different neural network architectures (SOM, feed-forward, RNN, GAN, Autoencoder/VAE, Transformer, etc.). The processes will be encapsulated in C/C++ libraries used to create open-source objects and modules in the Pure Data, Max, SuperCollider and Python languages/environments, applicable to real-time or offline processing contexts, integrating research and artistic creation flows such as Computer-Assisted Composition and Interactive Music Systems, among others. Expected results include: (1) the development and dissemination of flexible open-source (C/C++) real-time/offline tools and libraries/modules in Python, Pure Data, Max and SuperCollider; (2) at least 4 artistic pieces/creations involving the developed tools; (3) documentation and tutorials enabling application and contribution by the academic and artistic community. The research is linked to the activities of the Grupo de Pesquisa em Criação e Investigação Sonora (CrIS) and the Laboratório de Performance com Sistemas Interativos (LaPIS), both connected to the Centro de Pesquisa em Música Contemporânea (CPMC) of the UFMG School of Music, and to the Grupo Interdisciplinar de Pesquisa em Ciências e Tecnologias da Música e do Som.

  • Listening, studying and composing music with listening, learning and composition machines: a technical, creative and musicological investigation

    2023–2025 · José Henrique Padovani · CNPq

    Chamada Pública MCTI/CNPq nº 14/2023 - APOIO A PROJETOS INTERNACIONAIS DE PESQUISA CIENTÍFICA, TECNOLÓGICA E DE INOVAÇÃO

    abstract

    The project's object is the creative/investigative development and application of machine listening and machine learning techniques in the field of music, aiming to overcome current limitations of a development/application that is predominantly circumscribed and pre-formatted to commercial/pragmatic contexts such as musical source separation (demixing), similar-music recommendations and noise suppression. The research turns to technical, artistic and musicological investigation of tools and strategies for listening/learning/composition of/with machines, encompassing the design, development, application and evaluation of flexible machine listening/machine learning tools in collaborative creation and research contexts based on computational mediation in real time and offline. The technical development will use dynamic C/C++ libraries to create extensions across different environments and languages, resulting in a flexible architecture that adapts to processing time, type of application (real-time/offline) and environment/language (Pure Data, Max, SuperCollider and Python). The tools will be designed for specific applications in musical creation and musicology, based on a practical and collaborative methodology. The research hypothesizes that the collaborative and flexible exploration of machine listening and learning techniques in contemporary creative/investigative processes in the field of music can drive technological, artistic and musicological innovations. This hypothesis is supported by the history of collaborative technical/musicological/artistic development of tools such as Csound and Pure Data/Max, and of techniques such as FM synthesis and granular synthesis (all with significant impacts in innovation contexts). Mirroring such experiences, the project is structured around practice-based research methodologies and collaborative processes of artistic and musicological research.

  • Strategies for developing, adapting and applying machine listening/learning models and techniques in analytical-creative processes for instrumental, electroacoustic, audiovisual and interactive music

    2021–2025 · José Henrique Padovani · FAPEMIG

    Edital Nº 001/2021 - DEMANDA UNIVERSAL

    abstract

    This project proposes the investigation of strategies for developing, adapting and applying machine listening/learning models and techniques in the context of analytical and creative processes for instrumental, electroacoustic, audiovisual and interactive music. The goal is to develop strategies for applying these techniques specifically designed for analytical/creative processes in computer-mediated music in offline and real-time contexts. The methodology consists, in a first phase, of a review of texts, tools and combined machine listening/learning methods. In a second phase, processes specifically conceived for computer-assisted analysis/composition, for the creation/analysis of electroacoustic and audiovisual music and for situations involving interactive music systems will be implemented/adapted. After refining the developed strategies, the third phase involves their application in broader creative and analytical projects, serving at once as validation of the strategies and of the technological products related to them. The developed processes will be implemented both on widely used computational platforms (Python) and on open systems geared toward real-time music computing (Pure Data and SuperCollider). Of specific interest to the research are: 1. descriptors aimed at identifying attributes related to "timbre" (such as spectral centroid, spectral slope, spectral roll-off, spectral flux, spectral entropy, MFCC, BFCC, LPC, tristimulus, among others); and 2. machine learning processes related to model generation through regressions (from linear and polynomial to symbolic ones, via genetic programming), artificial neural networks, Bayesian networks, CNNs and RNNs. Results comprise theoretical-analytical works, artistic pieces and computational applications/libraries that consolidate the developed application strategies.

  • Study, development and application of real-time audiovisual techniques and processes in interactive music

    2021–2026 · José Henrique Padovani · CNPq

    Chamada CNPq/MCTI/FNDCT Nº 18/2021 - Faixa B - Grupos Consolidados

    abstract

    The project focuses on the study, development and application of flexible solutions aimed at the parameterization, identification, classification and computational processing of diverse sonic attributes in artistic contexts oriented, primarily, toward algorithmic composition and audiovisual live electronics (including interactive multimodal systems). These processes will be structured around the adaptation of machine listening/learning techniques — encompassing audio descriptors, signal analysis processes, statistical processes and various machine learning techniques — so as to allow their flexible application in real time and offline. Focusing primarily on (1) real-time music computing environments such as Pure Data, Max and SuperCollider, (2) creative computing environments/languages (Processing and openFrameworks), and (3) symbolic algorithmic composition environments (OpenMusic and Python, with the abjad and music21 libraries), the project should result in tools that allow flexible use from a creative standpoint, enabling everything from the design of interactive multimodal systems to the construction of compositional procedures usually associated with the field known as computer-assisted composition.

about

ecris@ecris.cc

The Espaço de Criação e Investigação Sonora (ECrIS — Space for Sound Creation and Research) is a research group, linked to the laboratory of the same name, dedicated to the cross-cutting processes of teaching, research and creation in music, sound art and interart/intermedia practices, spanning a wide range of practices, theories, methodologies and technologies.

The laboratory is part of the Center for Contemporary Music Research (CPMC) at the UFMG School of Music, and is based at Conservatório UFMG. There, the group promotes activities, events and working dynamics that bring together theory, practice and technology in contemporary research-creation processes. Its activities are closely tied to the Composition area of the undergraduate Music program and to the research lines "Analytical and Creative Processes" and "Sonology" of UFMG’s Graduate Program in Music, while also building cross-disciplinary and transdisciplinary links — collaborations and themes spanning Digital Arts, Computing, Communication and Philosophy.

ECrIS’s research and activities span investigation, invention and creation through a range of approaches: musical creation in its many forms (instrumental, electroacoustic, interactive, multimodal, experimental); creative and analytical processes mediated by technology (computational, generative, multimodal); the study of the epistemic, dialogical and sociocultural dimensions of sonic invention and of performance praxis; the development of computational techniques for sound synthesis, analysis and processing — including spatialization and machine listening; and interdisciplinary approaches drawing on fields such as sound ecology, phonetics and audio engineering.

With a strong vocation for interdisciplinarity, cross-disciplinary work and innovation, ECrIS aims to contribute to society and the academic community through artistic, bibliographic and technological output, in dialogue with fields such as philosophy, the humanities, ecology, linguistics, the arts, computing and engineering. This output takes shape not only as texts and artistic works, but also as concerts, performances, workshops and study groups — including in remote format.

ECrIS is coordinated by a team of four faculty members — José Henrique Padovani Velloso, Rogério Vasconcelos, Marco Scarassatti and Eduardo Campolina — rather than a single coordinator. Certified with Brazil’s CNPq research-group directory since it was formed in 2021, the group is affiliated with the Federal University of Minas Gerais (UFMG) and its Center for Contemporary Music Research (CPMC) at the School of Music, and is based at Conservatório UFMG.

research lines

  • Sound Ecology

    Theoretical, epistemological and practical dimensions of sound ecology — artistic creation, critical-discursive work and socially and environmentally engaged practice, connecting listening, reflection and action.

  • Machine Listening and Artificial Intelligence

    Technical, creative and critical research on technologies inspired by the perceptual-cognitive traits of humans and other beings, and their poetic, ethical and cultural impact on music.

  • Spatialization, Multimodality and Transmediality

    Creative, technical and theoretical aspects of sound spatialization, immersive processes and exploration across media — acousmatic, mixed/interactive, sound installations, audiovisual music.

  • Computer-Assisted Analytical and Creative Processes

    Development and application of algorithms and computational tools for musical composition, interactive systems, musical analysis, sonology and multimodal pedagogical contexts.

  • Research and Creation of Works for Orchestra and Large Ensembles

    Study and creation of orchestral and large-ensemble repertoire — analysis, timbre organization, the orchestra’s sociopolitical role and pedagogical proposals.

  • Theory, Epistemology and Praxis of Analytical and Creative Processes

    Theoretical, epistemological and practical aspects of contemporary approaches to musical and sonic analysis and creation, including critical-theoretical studies of compositions and creative practices.

  • Performance, Creation and Experimentation

    Individual or collective experimental performance projects integrating new musical and visual technologies, grounded in reflection on contemporary experimental thought.

  • Music and Discourse

    A theoretical framework linking musicology and language studies, with emphasis on dialogism and polyphony applied to composition, musical analysis and discourse analysis.

  • Digital Humanities and Data Science Applied to Research, Teaching and Creative Processes in Music

    Covers research that applies data mapping and digital humanities to research, teaching and artistic processes in music. It includes the collection, modeling and visualization of public data on graduate programs, research groups, projects, funding and bibliographic, artistic and technical output; the mapping of collaboration networks, themes, territories and international circulation; and the application of related methods in teaching and artistic creation contexts.
